Hiring Local Crew in New Jersey: What Incoming Productions Need to Know

Netflix, Lionsgate, and Paramount are all building in New Jersey. The crew is already here. Here is what production coordinators and line producers need to understand before they start making calls.

By Jay Franklin Director of Photography May 2026 New Jersey · Tristate

New Jersey is no longer a place productions pass through on their way to somewhere else. With Netflix breaking ground on a billion dollar facility at Fort Monmouth, Lionsgate building in Newark, and Paramount signing a ten year lease at 1888 Studios in Bayonne, the Garden State has positioned itself as one of the most significant production hubs in the country. The infrastructure is arriving. The question production coordinators and line producers are now asking is whether the crew is here to match it.

The answer is yes. But knowing where to look, what to expect, and how the New Jersey market works is different from knowing how to build a crew in Los Angeles or even in Manhattan. This is a practical guide for anyone coming into the New Jersey market to hire local.

The Market Has Changed Faster Than Most Realize

New Jersey went from 39 productions through its tax incentive program in 2023 to 63 in 2025. Crew hires nearly doubled in a single year, from 15,000 in 2023 to over 30,000 in 2024. That growth rate is not an accident. It is the result of a deliberately structured incentive program that offers up to 40 percent tax credits on qualified New Jersey production expenses, combined with the state's geographic advantage of being within reach of New York's talent pool while offering more competitive rates.

For production coordinators and line producers, this means the local crew market has been actively developing for several years. The people working in New Jersey now are not newcomers hoping to catch a wave. Many have been here through the growth period, building credits on real productions with real budgets.

The crew that built New Jersey's production reputation during the growth years is the same crew your production will benefit from hiring. Experience built locally is still experience.

What Local Hire Actually Means in New Jersey

Local hire in New Jersey is not the same conversation it would be in a smaller market. The Tristate region means that a DP or camera operator based in northern New Jersey has likely worked extensively in New York, has relationships across both markets, and brings a network that stretches across the region. When you hire local in New Jersey you are accessing Tristate experience, not just Garden State experience.

This matters practically. A local DP who has shot broadcast content for PBS and WNET, covered live events at Lincoln Center, and directed narrative work that screened at film festivals knows how to operate in professional production environments. That is not a regional credential. That is a professional one.

The Geography Works in Your Production's Favor

New Jersey's compact geography is one of its most underappreciated production advantages. You can move from urban environments in Newark to coastal locations, wooded areas, and small town streets within 90 minutes. For a production building varied location shooting into a schedule, this reduces travel days and keeps your crew costs manageable. Local crew who know those locations add genuine value to that equation because they can anticipate what a location requires before the scouts even arrive.

Roles to Fill Locally and Where to Find Them

The roles that make the most sense to fill locally in New Jersey center on camera, lighting, and grip departments, along with production coordination support and post production. These are areas where the local market has genuine depth built over years of active production.

Camera Department

Directors of Photography and camera operators with broadcast and narrative experience are available in New Jersey. Look for credits that include broadcast work, which indicates familiarity with professional production standards and deadline pressure, alongside narrative credits that demonstrate cinematic range. The best local DPs in this market have worked across both documentary and narrative formats, which is exactly the versatility a production moving across genres or formats needs.

Where to Start Your Search

The New Jersey Motion Picture and Television Commission maintains crew resources and can connect productions with local professionals. StaffMeUp and ProductionHub both have active New Jersey based crew listings. LinkedIn is increasingly where experienced local crew maintain professional profiles, and a search combining Director of Photography or Camera Operator with New Jersey will surface people who have been actively positioning for exactly this moment in the state's production growth.

The Tax Incentive and What It Means for Your Crew Budget

New Jersey's Film and Digital Media Tax Credit program, extended through 2049 in 2025 legislation, offers 30 to 40 percent credits on qualified production expenses. Wages paid to New Jersey crew qualify as eligible expenditures. This means that hiring local crew is not just a logistical convenience. It is a financial strategy that directly affects your production's credit eligibility.

Productions working within the 30 mile radius of midtown Manhattan qualify for a 30 percent base credit. Productions working further into the state, at facilities like the Fort Monmouth Netflix campus or the Bayonne 1888 Studios complex, qualify for up to 40 percent. Understanding where your production falls in that geography before you build your budget is essential.

Local Crew and the Qualified Expenditure Threshold

Every dollar paid to a New Jersey based crew member working on a qualifying production is a dollar that contributes to your qualified expenditure total. For productions trying to maximize their credit position, this makes the decision to hire locally not just practical but financially meaningful. A line producer who understands this dynamic will build local hiring into the budget from the beginning rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Hiring local in New Jersey is not just about convenience. It is a production finance decision with direct impact on your tax credit position.

What to Look for When Evaluating Local Crew

The same standards apply in New Jersey as anywhere else. Look for verifiable credits on productions of comparable scale. Look for experience across multiple formats if your production moves between them. Look for people who can name specific productions, specific roles, and specific challenges they navigated. A camera operator who has covered live events for major artists, worked broadcast for public television, and directed narrative work that competed at film festivals has demonstrated range across environments. That range is what keeps a production moving when conditions change, which they always do.

Ask about equipment. Local crew in the New Jersey market who have been working through the growth period are increasingly well equipped for professional production. Camera systems capable of 4K and 6K acquisition, FAA certified drone operators, and professionals with post production capabilities including color grading and broadcast delivery are available in this market. You do not always need to bring everything with you.

The Practical Conversation

The most useful thing a production coordinator can do when entering the New Jersey market is reach out early. Local crew who are serious about their work are not waiting passively. They are positioning, building relationships, and tracking which productions are coming. A conversation that starts three months before your shoot begins is always more productive than one that starts three weeks before.

New Jersey's production moment is real and it is happening now. The crew that helped build this market's reputation is here, working, credentialed, and ready for what comes next. The question is not whether the local talent exists. The question is whether you find them before someone else does.
