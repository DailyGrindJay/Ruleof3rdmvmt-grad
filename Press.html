The Film

A Story No One Thought to Tell.

Somewhere between the sidewalk and the storm drain, an ecosystem thrives unnoticed. Tumbleweave is a satirical mockumentary that follows Dr. La'ce Fruont, an independent urban naturalist who has dedicated his career to documenting what he calls "the most misunderstood organism in metropolitan America."

Through field observations, archival footage, and solemn voice-over, the film traces the complete life cycle of the hair weave, from its origins on the salon floor to its final resting place tangled in a chain-link fence on Broad Street.

With the sincerity of a nature documentary and the absurdity of something that absolutely should not be taken seriously, Tumbleweave asks the questions no one thought to ask: Where does it come from? Where does it go? And what does it say about the environments it passes through?

Rule of 3rd Mvmt Short Film Selected for International Movie Awards

Satirical mockumentary Tumbleweave earns festival recognition, marking a milestone for New Jersey independent filmmaker Jay Franklin.

Roselle, New Jersey — Rule of 3rd Mvmt, the New Jersey-based film and photography production company founded by filmmaker and cinematographer Jay Franklin, announces that its short film Tumbleweave has been selected as an official entry in the International Movie Awards.

The film, a satirical mockumentary following fictional urban naturalist Dr. La'ce Fruont as he documents the life cycle of the hair weave across metropolitan streets, blends the formal language of nature documentary filmmaking with absurdist social commentary. The result is a work that is simultaneously comedic, technically precise, and surprisingly resonant in what it reveals about the communities it observes.

Tumbleweave is part of the Showrunners short film series, an original production from Rule of 3rd Mvmt that demonstrates Franklin's range as both a director and a storyteller capable of working across genre, tone, and subject matter with equal command.

"This film started as a question," said Franklin. "What if you treated something completely ordinary like it mattered? The hair weave on the sidewalk. The drain. The fence. What does that environment say? What stories are already there that no one is looking for? That is the work. Finding the story that was always there."

With fourteen years of professional experience spanning broadcast television, political media, commercial brand production, and independent film, Franklin represents a generation of New Jersey filmmakers building serious creative infrastructure in a state increasingly recognized as a major destination for film and television production.
